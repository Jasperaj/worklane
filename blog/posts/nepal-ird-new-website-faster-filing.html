IRD's New Taxpayer Portal: What's New, and Is Filing Actually Faster?

Upaj Manandhar, Chartered Accountant Sep 8, 2026 9 min read

If you've heard that IRD "launched a new website," the accurate version is more specific: on Bhadra 9, 2083 (≈24–25 August 2026), IRD's Director General announced a rebuilt version of the taxpayer portal entering public trial — running in parallel with the existing site, not replacing it. ird.gov.np currently lists both करदाता पोर्टल and करदाता पोर्टल (नयाँ) as separate links. That distinction matters for how much you should rely on it right now.

Is it actually faster?

The stated goal — from IRD's own Director General — is services that are "easier, simpler, and more effective." That's an intent, not a measured result. We could not find any published downtime statistics, load-test data, or before/after speed comparison. Notably, IRD itself pre-emptively warned that "general technical problems may occur" during the trial — which is honest, but also tells you this is not yet a system anyone is claiming is faster under peak load.

The broader policy context is worth knowing too: Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle's FY 2083/84 budget speech pledged a "paperless, faceless, contactless" revenue administration with automated filing, payment, and refunds. The new portal appears to be a step toward that vision, but the budget speech is a policy commitment, not a specific claim about this portal release.

We searched specifically for post-launch complaints or bug reports from taxpayers and accountants and found none as of early September 2026 — which likely just means it's early days (roughly two weeks into trial at time of writing), not that the rollout has been trouble-free. We'll update this post as real usage feedback surfaces.

What to actually do this filing season

  1. Log into the new portal now — before a deadline, not during one — to confirm your credentials carry over and your historical data looks correct.
  2. Don't retire your old-portal habits yet. The old system remains live specifically because the new one is still in trial; keep both working until IRD confirms a full cutover.
  3. Spot-check auto-populated fields — particularly the new Schedule-10 auto-fill — against your own records rather than assuming a clean migration.
  4. If you're a foreign investor or need PAN/tax-clearance documents, check whether the new self-service downloads work for your case before making an office trip out of habit.
  5. Watch for the VAT return amendment feature specifically — see our VAT rectification guide for what we know so far.
