Advance Tax Installments in Nepal: The Section 94 Quarterly Payment Guide

Upaj Manandhar, Chartered Accountant Sep 8, 2026 9 min read

If your only income is a salary and your employer withholds TDS correctly, Section 94 doesn't apply to you — your tax is already being paid as you earn. But the moment you have business or investment income, Nepal expects you to pay tax during the year in three installments, not as one lump sum after year-end. Missing this is one of the more expensive oversights we see, because the penalty compounds monthly.

Who has to pay, and the threshold

Section 94 of the Income Tax Act 2058 applies to anyone with assessable income from business or investment — companies, partnerships, self-employed professionals, and anyone with rental or investment income above the threshold. Advisory sources commonly cite an NPR 7,500 estimated-tax exemption — below this, no installment obligation arises. We also found the statutory text itself referencing a smaller NPR 2,000 per-installment de minimis figure; these may be two distinct thresholds rather than a contradiction, but confirm the exact figure applicable to your case before relying on either number.

The 40/70/100 schedule

Cumulative percentage of estimated annual tax due at each installment date

These percentages are cumulative, not additive — by Chaitra-end you need 70% of your estimated annual tax paid in total, not 70% on top of the earlier 40%.

Estimating — and revising — your tax

"Estimated tax" is your own projection of annual liability, recalculated at each installment date against current information, not a figure you lock in once at the start of the year. Already-paid tax offsetting each installment includes prior installments, TDS already withheld under Chapter 17, amounts remitted under Section 90, and medical-expense tax credits under Section 51. If your income picture changes — a big new contract, a slow quarter — revise the estimate at the next installment date rather than waiting until year-end to true it up.

The cost of getting it wrong

Section 118 gives you a 90% safe harbor: if your cumulative installments by year-end reach at least 90% of your actual final tax liability, no fee applies — even if your original estimate was off. Fall short of 90%, and a fee accrues at the "normal rate of interest" per month (or part-month) from each underpaid installment's due date through the date of final assessment. Advisory sources commonly cite this rate around 15% per annum, but we could not independently confirm the exact statutory percentage — confirm the current figure before calculating an exposure estimate for a client.

The mechanism runs both ways: overpay past the 90% mark and Section 118(3) provides for interest back to you on the excess, calculated the same way.

How to actually pay

The general e-payment flow on the IRD Taxpayer Portal: log in with your PAN, password and CAPTCHA → go to Payments → select the relevant tax type/schedule → enter the amount → generate a Payment Reference Number (PRN) → settle it via Connect IPS, a digital wallet (eSewa/Khalti), or by presenting the PRN at an authorised bank counter → confirm under Payment History. We could not confirm the exact screen-by-screen steps specific to the installment schedule (referenced in some sources as form D-02) — if your local IRO uses a different path, follow their guidance over this general flow.

What changed for FY 2083/84

The core Section 94 mechanics — the 40/70/100 schedule, the dates, the 90% safe harbor — are unchanged. Two narrower changes from Finance Act 2083 are worth knowing:

  • Ride-sharing/platform operators now carry a 1% advance-tax collection duty on payments made to their service providers.
  • Advance-tax rates on disposal of listed securities (Section 95Ka) were revised — short-holding individual rates increased and brackets re-rated, with a new concessional 2.5% rate for involuntary land disposals.
Also relevant: FY 2083/84's slab overhaul (1% band now up to 10 lakh, top rate 29%) changes what your "estimated tax" should actually be this year. See our full slab breakdown before you calculate your next installment.
