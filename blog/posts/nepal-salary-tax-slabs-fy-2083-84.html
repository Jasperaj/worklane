Nepal Salary Tax Slabs FY 2083/84: Full Table, Deductions and Worked Examples

Upaj Manandhar, Chartered Accountant Sep 8, 2026 11 min read

Our earlier post on FY 2082/83 slabs flagged a "unified schedule" as still a proposal for FY 2083/84. It's no longer a proposal — the Economic (Finance) Bill 2083 passed on 19 June 2026, and IRD's own natural-person tax-rate notice (22 Shrawan 2083) confirms these are the rates in force for the fiscal year that began 17 July 2026. This post replaces the old proposal numbers with the confirmed ones.

The unified slab table

For the first time, one table applies regardless of marital status — the old separate individual/couple schedules are gone.

Taxable income (annual)Rate
Up to NPR 1,000,0001% (Social Security Tax band)
1,000,001 – 1,500,00010%
1,500,001 – 2,500,00020%
2,500,001 – 4,000,00027%
Above 4,000,00029%
Each bar is the rate applied to income within that band, not the average rate on total income

Compared to FY 2082/83: the 1% band doubled from 5 lakh (individual) / 6 lakh (couple) to a flat 10 lakh for everyone; the 30% bracket was eliminated entirely; and the top rate dropped ten full points, from 39% to 29%.

Deductions before the slabs apply

  • SSF contribution: if you contribute to the Social Security Fund (or an approved retirement fund), the 1% band on the first 10 lakh is waived — you move straight to 10% on the portion above your SSF-exempt amount.
  • Life insurance premium: deductible up to NPR 40,000.
  • Health/medical insurance premium: deductible up to NPR 20,000.
  • Housing insurance: deductible up to NPR 10,000.
  • Remote-area allowance: NPR 10,000–50,000 depending on area category.
  • Medical tax credit: up to NPR 1,500 or 15% of approved treatment cost (not combinable with the health insurance deduction).
  • Disability rebate: the 1% threshold rises by 50% (effectively 15 lakh instead of 10 lakh) for a taxpayer with disability.
  • Retirement fund contributions (SSF/CIT/PF): deductible up to the lower of one-third of income or NPR 500,000.
  • Female tax rebate: retained at 10% of total tax payable for a resident woman earning only employment income.
One nuance we flagged during research and haven't fully resolved: a footnote in at least one CA-firm summary suggests the 1% concessional band may exclude sole proprietors receiving pension income. If you're advising a retired client, confirm this specifically before relying on the 1% band for their pension income.

Worked examples

Take a resident individual earning NPR 1,200,000 a year:

Not contributing to SSF 1% × 1,000,000 = NPR 10,000
10% × 200,000 (the slice from 10L–12L) = NPR 20,000
Total tax = NPR 30,000 (NPR 27,000 if the 10% female rebate applies)
Contributing to SSF (1% band waived) 10% × 200,000 = NPR 20,000 (NPR 18,000 with the female rebate)

SSF contribution alone cuts this taxpayer's liability by a third — worth checking your own withholding setup if you haven't already.

What this means for payroll right now

  1. Update payroll software or Excel formulas to the unified table — remove any individual/couple branching logic.
  2. Re-run TDS on salaries paid since 17 July 2026 (Shrawan 1, 2083) if withholding was still calculated on the old slabs.
  3. Confirm the female rebate is still being applied correctly — it wasn't removed, but check your payroll system wasn't hardcoded against the old table structure.
  4. If you manage payroll in Excel, see our Excel payroll workbook guide for how to rebuild the tax formula cleanly.
