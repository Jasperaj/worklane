Nepal's FY 2083/84 Budget and the New IRD Portal: What Actually Changed

Upaj Manandhar, Chartered Accountant Sep 8, 2026 11 min read

Two things happened to Nepal's tax system going into FY 2083/84 that matter for every filing this year: the Finance Act rewrote the income tax slabs, and IRD quietly put a rebuilt version of the taxpayer portal into public trial. We're covering both here, plus untangling a specific question we kept getting asked — what exactly is the "D-01 live update" people have heard about.

The headline change: slabs unified, top rate cut to 29%

The Economic (Finance) Bill 2083 was passed by the House of Representatives on 19 June 2026 — before FY 2083/84 began on 17 July 2026 — and IRD's own natural-person tax-rate notice (dated 22 Shrawan 2083) confirms these rates are in force, not proposals under discussion.

The individual and couple schedules are now one unified table — the old separate-rates-for-married-couples system is gone. The 1% band doubled from 5 lakh to 10 lakh, the 30% bracket was scrapped entirely, and the top rate dropped ten points, from 39% to 29%. We've broken down deductions, the female rebate, and worked examples in a dedicated post — see Nepal Salary Tax Slabs FY 2083/84: Full Table, Deductions and Worked Examples.

Other Finance Act 2083 changes worth knowing

  • VAT stays at 13% as the standard rate, but two new carve-outs apply: 5% VAT on residential electricity above 50 units per billing cycle, and 5% VAT on ride-hailing services (see our earlier post on Nepal's Multi-Rate VAT for the compliance playbook).
  • A 10% instant VAT refund for purchases made via digital payment.
  • A universal VAT-bill lottery — every VAT invoice is now a draw entry, intended to push consumers to demand bills.
  • Advance-tax collection duties extended to ride-sharing/platform operators (1% on payments to service providers).
  • Revised advance-tax rates on disposal of listed securities, including a new concessional 2.5% rate for involuntary land disposals.

The IRD portal enters trial

Separately from the Finance Act, IRD's Director General announced a rebuilt version of the taxpayer portal entering public trial on Bhadra 9, 2083 (≈24–25 August 2026) — built, per IRD's own description, on "design-driven development" and a microservice architecture. As of this writing, ird.gov.np lists both the old करदाता पोर्टल and a new करदाता पोर्टल (नयाँ) as separate links — this is a parallel run, not a completed cutover, and IRD itself has pre-emptively warned that "general technical problems may occur" during testing.

Confirmed new capabilities on the trial portal:

  • Self-service download of your own PAN card, excise licence, and tax clearance certificate — no office visit required.
  • Self-verification of certain statements online, removing an IRO staff sign-off step.
  • Monthly TDS/withholding statements submittable directly by taxpayers.
  • Online PAN registration for foreign investors.
  • Auto-population of Schedule-10 income-return entries, rather than manual entry.
  • A VAT return amendment service — the first sign of an actual online correction path, which we cover in detail in our VAT return rectification guide.

We could not find any public data on downtime or reliability comparisons between the old and new portal, no confirmed Nagarik App/National ID single sign-on tied to this release, and no confirmed software/API access for accountants — treat "faster filing" as the stated intent for now, not yet a measured result.

So what is "D-01," really?

We looked into the specific term "D-01 live update" that's been circulating, and we want to be straight about what we found: no IRD circular, notice, or news report uses that exact phrase. Sources disagree even on what "D-01" refers to — some describe it as the presumptive-taxpayer return (small businesses under NPR 30 lakh turnover paying a flat fixed tax), while an IRD SOP document title suggests it might instead label the standard self-assessment income tax return. We could not resolve this conflict from public documents alone.

Our best read: the "live update" people are describing is most likely the Schedule-10 auto-population feature on the new trial portal — income-return entries that used to require manual entry now populate automatically from filed data. If you've heard the term used differently, or have logged into the new portal and can confirm which form D-01 actually is, we'd genuinely like to know — get in touch.

What to do differently this filing season

  1. Recompute withholding and estimated tax using the unified slab table — don't carry over last year's individual/couple split.
  2. If you're on the new trial portal, check whether your Schedule-10 entries auto-populated correctly before you rely on them — verify against your own records rather than assuming the migration was clean.
  3. If you need to correct a filed VAT return, check the new portal's amendment service first before assuming you need to visit your Inland Revenue Office in person.
  4. Keep your login working on both portals for now — the old one remains live, and trial systems occasionally roll back.
