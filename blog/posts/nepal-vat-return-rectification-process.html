How to Rectify a Filed VAT Return in Nepal: Step-by-Step

Upaj Manandhar, Chartered Accountant Sep 8, 2026 10 min read

Every accountant hits this eventually: a VAT return is already submitted on the IRD Taxpayer Portal and then someone spots the error — a wrong sales figure, a purchase entered under the wrong tax period, a duplicate invoice. The instinct is to look for an "edit" button. There isn't one, and understanding why changes how you should actually handle it.

Why there's no simple online correction

Nepal's VAT system is self-assessment — you calculate and file your own liability, and IRD's portal help material for the standard e-filing flow is explicit about what happens after you click submit: "the data have already been submitted and you cannot change." You can edit a saved-but-not-yet-submitted return freely. Once it's submitted, the correction path moves outside the self-service portal — into either IRD's own reassessment power (VAT Act 2052, Sections 20/23) or, as of the portal's 2026 trial rebuild, a newly listed "VAT return amendment" service whose exact steps aren't yet publicly documented in detail.

The practical correction path, pieced together from the VAT Act and portal behaviour

Step-by-step: what to actually do

  1. Confirm the return status Log in and check whether the affected return is still in "saved" state or already "submitted." This determines everything that follows — a saved-but-unsubmitted return is a non-issue, just correct and submit it.
  2. Check the new trial portal first If your Inland Revenue Office has moved you onto the rebuilt taxpayer portal (in trial since Bhadra 2083), look for a "VAT return amendment" option before doing anything else — this is a genuinely new service, though we haven't yet found a published screen-by-screen walkthrough for it. If it's there and works, this is now your fastest path.
  3. If no online option exists, prepare a written application Draft an application addressed to your local Inland Revenue Office explaining the error, the correct figures, and the tax period affected. Attach your revised purchase/sales register and the underlying invoices supporting the correction. There's no officially published template for this letter — keep it factual, dated, and signed by an authorised person.
  4. Expect an IRD-initiated review, not an instant fix Under Sections 20 and 23 of the VAT Act 2052, IRD can issue a notice requesting information, give you 15 days to respond with evidence, and then pass a final order under Rule 29(3) of the VAT Rules 2053. This is the same mechanism IRD uses when it initiates a correction — your application effectively triggers the same process from the other direction.
  5. Know your deadline An amended assessment for a tax period must generally be made within 4 years of the return's due date. That limit does not apply in cases of fraud.
  6. If you disagree with the outcome You can seek administrative review within 30 days (extendable by another 30), which requires depositing 25% of the disputed tax, interest, and fine. Beyond that, appeal to the Revenue Tribunal requires a 50% deposit (net of what you already paid at the review stage).
On penalties: Late filing carries a fee of NPR 1,000 per tax period or 0.05% per day, whichever is higher. Interest on underpaid VAT is commonly cited around 15% per annum, but this figure moves with each year's Finance Act and we found conflicting secondary sources for the current rate — confirm the exact current percentage with us or directly against ird.gov.np before relying on it for a calculation.

A note on images

IRD has published screen-by-screen walkthroughs for original VAT return filing, but as of this writing we could not find an equivalent official walkthrough with screenshots for the correction/amendment process specifically — likely because, until the new portal's trial, correction genuinely wasn't a self-service action. We'll update this post with portal screenshots once the amendment service is confirmed stable and documented.
